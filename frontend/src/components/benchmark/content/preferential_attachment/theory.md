# Theory

Preferential Attachment is both a link prediction algorithm and a fundamental generative mechanism for complex networks. First formally described by Derek de Solla Price in 1976 for citation networks (as "cumulative advantage") and later popularized by Albert-László Barabási and Réka Albert in 1999 for scale-free networks, it embodies the "rich get richer" or "Matthew effect" principle: nodes with many existing connections are more likely to acquire new connections.

## Key Concepts

- **Rich Get Richer**: High-degree nodes attract more connections over time
- **Linear Preference**: Connection probability is proportional to current degree
- **Scale-Free Networks**: Networks generated by this process exhibit power-law degree distributions
- **Cumulative Advantage**: Early advantages compound over time
- **Multiplicative Score**: PA(u,v) = deg(u) × deg(v)
- **Parameter-Free**: Simple product of degrees, no tuning needed

## Mathematical Foundation

### Link Prediction Score

For nodes u and v, the preferential attachment score is:

$$PA(u, v) = |\Gamma(u)| \times |\Gamma(v)|$$

Where $|\Gamma(u)|$ is the degree of node u.

### Generative Model (Barabási-Albert)

The network grows as follows:
1. Start with a small initial graph of $m_0$ nodes
2. At each time step, add a new node with $m$ edges ($m \leq m_0$)
3. Each new edge connects to existing node $i$ with probability:

$$P(i) = \frac{k_i}{\sum_j k_j}$$

where $k_i$ is the current degree of node $i$.

### Emergent Properties

After $t$ time steps:
- **Degree Distribution**: $P(k) \sim k^{-3}$ (power law with exponent 3)
- **Average Path Length**: $L \sim \frac{\log N}{\log \log N}$ (ultra-small world)
- **Clustering Coefficient**: Decays as $N^{-0.75}$
- **Assortativity**: Neutral or slightly disassortative

### Relation to Other Models

| Model | Mechanism | Degree Distribution |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| Erdős-Rényi | Random attachment | Poisson (exponential tail) |
| Watts-Strogatz | Rewiring for clustering | Poisson-like |
| Barabási-Albert | Preferential attachment | Power law ($\gamma = 3$) |
| Price Model | Cumulative advantage | Power law ($\gamma = 2 + 1/m$) |
| Fitness Model | Fitness + PA | Power law with exponent depending on fitness distribution |

## Historical Context

The concept has roots going back to:
- **1925**: Udny Yule describes "Yule process" for species evolution
- **1955**: Herbert Simon explains power laws in wealth, city sizes, word frequencies
- **1965**: Derek Price applies to citation networks ("cumulative advantage")
- **1999**: Barabási & Albert formalize the scale-free network model
- **2000s**: Extensive validation across biological, social, and technological networks

## Applications in Link Prediction

Preferential attachment is particularly effective for:
- **Growing Networks**: Social networks where new users connect to popular users
- **Citation Networks**: New papers cite highly-cited papers
- **Collaboration Networks**: New researchers collaborate with established scientists
- **Biological Networks**: Protein interaction networks expand through gene duplication
- **Web Graphs**: New pages link to popular, high-traffic pages
- **Infrastructure Networks**: New airports connect to major hubs

## Limitations

Despite its elegance, preferential attachment has significant limitations:
- **Ignores Common Neighbors**: Only considers degrees, not shared connections
- **Linear Assumption**: Real networks often show sub-linear or super-linear preference
- **Zero for Isolated Nodes**: Nodes with zero degree always get score 0
- **No Community Structure**: Generated networks lack strong communities
- **No Triadic Closure**: Mechanism doesn't capture friendship formation through common friends
- **Static View**: Doesn't consider temporal evolution of node popularity
- **No Node Similarity**: Purely based on popularity, not similarity or complementarity

## Theoretical Variations

### Non-Linear Preferential Attachment
$$PA(u,v) = |\Gamma(u)|^\alpha \times |\Gamma(v)|^\alpha$$

- $\alpha < 1$: Sub-linear — large hubs lose advantage
- $\alpha = 1$: Standard linear preference
- $\alpha > 1$: Super-linear — "winner takes all" behavior

### Generalized Preferential Attachment
Includes both additive and multiplicative components:
$$PA(u,v) = (|\Gamma(u)| + c) \times (|\Gamma(v)| + c)$$

The constant $c$ prevents zero scores for new nodes.

## Empirical Evidence

Research has confirmed preferential attachment in:
- **Citation Networks**: Scientific papers, patents
- **Social Media**: Twitter following, Facebook friendships (controversial)
- **Metabolic Networks**: Protein interaction networks
- **Internet**: Autonomous System (AS) level topology
- **Collaboration**: Actor collaborations, scientific co-authorship
- **Transportation**: Airline route networks, subway expansion

However, recent studies suggest that in many social networks, triadic closure and homophily may be stronger predictors than pure preferential attachment.
